My Plan · Built from two masterclasses

Regulate Better + End Defensiveness

Built from End of Defensiveness, Emotional Regulation for Married Men, and my own trigger map. This is what I'm actually going to do. The working copy lives in my daily doc.

01

The job

I'm not trying to never feel defensive.

I'm training myself to:

  • Catch it earlier.
  • Create a gap.
  • Stay instead of escaping.
  • Understand before I defend.

When I notice the heat rising, the anger boiling towards the surface, my stomach dropping or my jaw tightening:

Feet down.
One breath.

What did she actually say, and what did my trigger make me hear?

What is she protecting?

Stay. Listen. Don't fix. Don't defend.

02

My trigger map

The six boxes from the masterclass exercise, worked into what I'm actually going to do

1My #1 Situational Trigger
What sets me off
Being told directly that I've fallen short.

"You minimised this."
"You did a bad job with that check-in."
"I shouldn't have to ask for this."
"You're not making me feel safe."
2My #1 Relational Trigger
What makes it hit harder
Chez's tone when she's frustrated, angry or sarcastic.

The cheating coming up unexpectedly.

It's the ambush feeling. I'm suddenly in something emotionally loaded that I wasn't prepared for.
3My #1 Historical Trigger
What it hits underneath
The cheating and other failures can hit the belief:

"I'm a bad person."
"I have no integrity."
"I'm failing as a partner."
"I'm not who I think I am."

That belief can be what I HEAR even when it isn't what she SAID.

So ask:

"What did I just hear?"

"What did she actually say?"

Don't defend against a sentence she didn't say.
4My Body Signal
How I know I'm heating up
I feel the heat rising in my face.

My stomach drops.

My jaw starts clenching or I grind my teeth.

I can feel the anger boiling towards the surface and the urge to do something with it: bite back, explain myself, prove my point or get out of the conversation.

This is the moment I'm learning to catch earlier.

It's not proof that I'm being attacked.
It's not proof that she's right.
It's not proof that I'm bad.

It's the signal that I've been triggered.
5My Default Reaction
My escape routes
Bite back.
Get openly frustrated.
Explain.
Correct.
Defend.
Shut down.

These all get me out of the discomfort in different ways.

My new move is STAY.
6My Commitment
What I'm practising instead
Before I respond:

Plant my feet.
Take one breath.

Then ask, stay, respond. Full steps in the box below.
My Commitment, continued

Ask:

"What did she actually say, and what did my trigger make me hear?"

Separate what Chez actually said from what my trigger made it mean.

Did she actually say I'm a bad person, I've failed her, I have no integrity, I don't care, or I'm doing a terrible job?

Or did what she said hit the wound underneath, and my brain filled in the rest?

Then ask:

"What is she protecting?"

Don't get stuck on the anger, frustration, tone or exact words. Look for what might be underneath them.

Is she hurt?
Scared?
Feeling unsafe?
Feeling unimportant?
Worried she can't trust me?
Feeling like I still don't understand the impact?

Don't dismiss what she's saying as "she's just triggered" or "she's lashing out."

Whatever the delivery looks like, try to understand the hurt, fear or need underneath it.

Then STAY.

Let her finish, even when I want to jump in.

Don't explain my intention yet.
Don't correct the details yet.
Don't tell her why I did it.
Don't try to make the discomfort go away.

Listen until I understand what this is like for her.

Then respond.

Understand first. My side can come after.

03

Outside the moment

The pressure doesn't start when Chez says something.

I can already be carrying frustration, anger, resentment, disappointment or other shit from my day or my life.

If I don't process it, I hold onto it. The pressure builds. Then something with Chez can become the place I let it out.

The job is to notice what I'm carrying before that happens.

04

Daily journal: if something comes up

I already do:

  • What went well?
  • What didn't go well?
  • What can I improve on?

I don't need another daily journaling exercise.

But if I notice anger, frustration, resentment or something still bothering me, I don't just write "I got pissed off at AI today" and move on.

I use this:

Am I holding onto something?
  • What happened? Facts only.
  • What am I actually feeling about it?
  • What am I telling myself about it?
  • What am I still holding onto?
  • Where do I feel it in my body?
  • What's underneath the anger? What is the anger protecting?
  • Is there something I haven't admitted because it feels petty, selfish, embarrassing or unreasonable?

Finish with:

"Right now, I'm holding onto __________."

That's enough.

I don't have to fix it.
I don't have to justify it.
I don't have to decide whether I should feel this way.

I need to get it out instead of letting it quietly build.

05

Sit with it: 5 minutes

After journaling, especially if something has come up:

Feet down.

Breathe.

Name it plainly:

"Right now I'm angry about ______."
"I'm disappointed about ______."
"I'm holding onto ______."

Find where I feel it in my body.

Stay there.

When my head starts explaining, judging, solving or building the case, come back to the physical feeling.

Don't make it disappear.

Don't talk myself out of it.

Let myself actually feel the fucking thing instead of carrying it into the next conversation.

Ask once:

"What is this protecting?"

Wait.

Write one line if something comes up.

Done.
