01

The one line

If I only remember one thing

Plant my feet. One breath. What is she protecting?

Then stay. Listen, don't fix, don't defend.

My trigger map, short version: being told directly that I fell short, her tone and the ambush of old things coming up, and the belief that gets poked underneath. It shows up as heat in the face, a stomach drop and a clenched jaw. I bite back or shut down. The full map is at the top of the Emotional Regulation page.

"What is she protecting?" is the real question. Her anger is usually covering something more exposed: fear, not feeling safe, or not trusting that it won't happen again.

03

Journal prompts

For holding on to things: anger about not doing a good job, and about where life is at

Pick one a day. Write it, don't fix it. The point of the page is to get what I'm carrying out of my head so it stops running the day.

  1. What am I holding onto right now? One sentence, no explaining.
  2. Where is it in my body?
  3. Who is this anger pointed at: what happened today, or me?
  4. What standard am I measuring myself against? Who set it? Would I give it to someone else?
  5. Facts only: what did I say I'd do this week, and what did I do? No grade, no story.
  6. Where is my life at compared with where I thought it would be by now? Name the specific gap. Which one piece can I move this week?
  7. What is the anger protecting?
  8. What haven't I said out loud yet that I'd rather not say, and to who?

Once a week, 10 minutes: write the sentence my head says when I get defensive (for example "I'm a bad person" or "I'm doing a terrible job"). Then write the facts for it and the facts against it. Take that sentence to a BRN session focus or to Jade. This is the belief that gets poked, and it needs the identity-level work, not just tools.

04

Sit with it

Focused meditation on what I'm holding, and practice sitting in discomfort

Right after the journal, 5 to 10 minutes.

  1. Settle. Feet down. 4 in, hold 4, out 4, hold 4. Two minutes.
  2. Name it: "Right now I'm angry about ___."
  3. Find it in my body. Put my attention there. Don't change it.
  4. Stay for three minutes. When the story starts ("I did a bad job", "my life is..."), go back to the sensation. The job is to stay, not to fix it or feel better.
  5. Ask once: what is this protecting? Wait. Write one line.
  6. One breath. Done.
The ladder: build it, don't force it

10-minute sit. Use the phrases with Chez in ordinary conversations, not only tense ones.
05

In the moment

The five steps

1
Catch the body signal
Heat in the face, stomach drop, jaw.
2
One breath, feet planted
Six seconds. Physiological sigh if I need it: two quick breaths in, one long out.
3
Absorb
Let her finish. Don't correct the facts yet. Ask: what did I just hear, and what was actually said?
4
Get curious
Tell me more. What is she protecting?
5
Then respond
If it's too much, say so, give a time, and come back.

If it's too much

"I'm getting worked up and I don't want to react badly. I need ten minutes and then I'll come back." Give a real time and keep it. Going silent and leaving is the shutdown I'm trying to avoid.

The three questions for this pattern

  1. What am I about to do? (bite back, or shut down)
  2. What do I usually do next? (explain, deflect, go silent)
  3. What would my next level self do instead? (breath, what is she protecting, stay)

When "you're doing a terrible job" lands: check whether it's her words or the belief underneath. The words are usually smaller than what I heard.

06

Words to have ready

Prompts for my memory, not a script. Use her own words. Change anything that doesn't sound like me.

To slow it down
Yeah. Okay.
Hang on, I want to get this right.
Say that again, I'm listening.
To show I got it
Sounds like it felt like ___. (her words)
Yeah, that would've stung.
I didn't see it like that.
To ask for the real thing
What was the worst part of that for you?
What did you need from me right then?
Did it feel like I wasn't there?
To own it (only if it's true)
I got that wrong.
I need ten minutes. I'm coming back.
07

After a rupture

Review it before the repair conversation, not after

60 seconds the same day, then write it out before I talk to her. Facts, one line each. What happened, and my part. Then the three columns:

Ownership
What I own. Only what's true.
Impact
What it was like for her, in her words, not mine.
Changed action
One specific thing I'll do differently.

Then talk. Open with the ownership line. Don't relitigate the argument. Ask what she needs. If my mind goes blank when I go back, the page does the thinking, because critical thinking goes offline when I'm emotional.

Game tape: once a week, compare the last blow-up or near miss with the ones before it. Sleep, work stress, time of day, was I over-explaining or minimising. Patterns show up. Then talk them through with her instead of white-knuckling it.

08

Tell Chez

One calm conversation, not in the middle of an argument

"I'm working on not getting defensive when you bring something to me. I'm going to take a breath before I answer, so I might go quiet for a few seconds. I'm listening. If it's too much I'll say so, give you a time, and come back. What would help you most when you're upset: me listening, or me helping fix it?"

If she raises several things at once: "Can we take one at a time so I can hear each one properly?" (from End of Defensiveness).

09

Weekly review: three extra lines

Added to the review I already do

  1. How many times did the trigger fire this week?
  2. Did the one line run (breath, what is she protecting)? What happened?
  3. Game tape: the last blow-up or near miss. Sleep, work stress, time of day. Was I over-explaining or minimising?

A miss is data, not a verdict.

10

What this doesn't do

Tools handle the moment

The tools buy space and give me something to do with it. The sentence underneath ("bad person", "terrible job") is what gets poked, and that gets worked in the NLH and BRN work with Jade. That's where the weekly belief sentence goes.
